                   2026 Ignite Design Challenge
                                        Salesforce
 Lead Prioritization: The Right Lead, to the Right Rep, at the
                          Right Time
                    Focus area: Computer Science / Software Engineering




Challenge overview
Sales organizations receive prospective customer information from websites, events, and
referrals. A core operational challenge is deciding which leads deserve immediate attention and
which representative should receive them based on their region, expertise, language, and
current workload. Teams must build a streamlined software prototype that takes in large lists of
leads, calculates a priority score based on business rules, assigns leads to the most appropriate
representative, and displays the results on a simple manager-facing dashboard.

Background
Today, much of this work is done manually or inefficiently. Each sales lead comes with
information such as company size, recent activity, industry, region, and language, while
representatives differ in expertise, seniority, and current workload. A reliable routing system
must account for these differences without overloading representatives or overlooking important
leads.
The incoming datasets simulate real-world, messy data containing inconsistent formatting,
missing values, and unstandardized aliases. Teams will need to prepare these records before
the routing logic can successfully match leads to representatives. The finished system should
also make it easy for a sales manager to review and understand the results.

Objective
Your team must build a streamlined software prototype to prioritize and route sales leads using
provided datasets of leads and sales representatives. You will design a working system that
cleans incoming data, calculates lead priority scores, assigns leads to the most appropriate
representatives, and displays the results on a manager-facing dashboard. The system must
also handle missing data and sudden large uploads without failing.

Success should include:
• A rule-based engine that assigns point values to leads and groups them into Critical, High,
Medium, and Low priority levels, including VIP overrides.
• Smart routing based on Region, Language, Industry Vertical, representative seniority, and
available capacity.
• A thoughtful distribution strategy that balances assignments and sends overflow leads to a
visible Manager Review queue.
• A lightweight, interactive dashboard showing the prioritized lead queue, system health metrics,
and representative capacity.
• A working system that processes the large-scale dataset quickly and handles missing values,
invalid inputs, and other edge cases.

Given information and assumptions
• Two pairs of CSV datasets are provided: 100 sample leads with 10 representatives for initial
development, and 50,000-scale leads with 1,600 representatives for performance testing.
• Lead records include region, language, industry vertical, company size, recent activity, and a
VIP referral flag. Representative records include region, language, industry expertise, seniority,
current capacity, and maximum capacity.
• The Scoring Logic Cheat Sheet provides baseline point values and priority tiers. Teams may
refine or redesign the standard scoring model if they explain their reasoning. VIP referrals must
still receive Critical priority.
• Representative capacity starts at zero in the provided datasets. Teams must respect
maximum-capacity limits and handle invalid or missing values without causing the system to fail.
• The Data Dictionary explains field definitions and expected values. Records may contain
inconsistent casing, aliases, missing information, or ambiguous values. Teams should
document reasonable assumptions and fallback strategies.

Requirements and constraints
Requirements
   1. Data Quality: The incoming datasets simulate real-world, messy data containing
      inconsistent formatting, missing values, and unstandardized aliases. Your system must
      programmatically clean, standardize, and prepare these strings before the routing logic
      can successfully match leads to representatives.
   2. Automated Scoring: Create a rule-based engine that assigns point values to leads
      based on traits (company size, recent activity, industry) and groups them into priority
      levels (Critical, High, Medium, Low) according to the provided Scoring Logic Cheat
      Sheet.
   3. VIP Override: Leads flagged as VIP_Referral must bypass standard scoring and
      immediately be categorized as "Critical".
   4. Missing Data Fallback: When a lead is missing a critical field, the system must not fail.
      Teams must determine and implement their own logical fallback strategy to ensure the
      prospect is appropriately handled and not dropped.

   5. Smart Routing (Multi-Skill Matrix): Assign leads to representatives based on a strict
      matching of Region, Language, and Industry Vertical.
   6. Seniority Constraint: "Critical" priority leads are highly sensitive. The system must
      attempt to route them to a "Senior" representative first. If all eligible Seniors are at
      maximum capacity, the logic should then route to a "Manager". "Junior" representatives
      cannot receive Critical leads.
   7. Round-Robin Distribution: If multiple representatives share the exact same matching
      criteria and have available capacity, teams must design and implement their own
      distribution logic. Do not simply assign all leads to the first matching record. You are
      encouraged to apply computer science scheduling algorithms or custom allocation
      strategies, and you must be prepared to defend your chosen approach during the
      presentation.
   8. The Manager Review Queue (Overflow): If every eligible representative reaches their
      maximum capacity limit, do not drop the remaining leads. Route them to a visible
      "Manager Review" overflow bucket.
   9. Scale Challenge: The system must be capable of processing a sudden, massive upload
      of new leads (leads_scale.csv) quickly without freezing. Automated tests should also
      demonstrate that the core logic handles edge cases correctly.
Dashboard & UI Requirements
• Interactive System: Build a lightweight frontend or visual dashboard to display the routing
engine's outputs and system status in real time. Use your best judgment to design a clean,
responsive layout that effectively presents these controls and displays system outcomes.
     • Execution Triggers: Provide controls to run your pipeline against both the provided
     sample and scale datasets on demand.
     • State Reset: Include a way to clear pipeline outputs and reset representative capacities
     back to baseline.
• System Health KPIs: Teams must determine which metrics are most critical for end-users
(such as sales managers) to monitor. Design your own dashboard metric cards that highlight the
operational and system health data you believe is most valuable, and be prepared to defend
why you chose those metrics.
• Prioritized Lead Queue: Teams must design an intuitive interface displaying the routed
results. Use your product engineering intuition and user empathy to determine the best way for
a sales manager to interact with this data. Consider what features (such as sorting, filtering, or
grouping) are necessary for a manager to effectively oversee a high-stakes lead queue, and be
prepared to explain your UX decisions.
• Capacity & Load Tracking: Provide a visual way to track lead assignments against
representative bandwidth (e.g., allocated load vs. maximum capacity per representative). Use
your best judgment to present representative utilization clearly and compactly without cluttering
the interface.
Constraints
• Strict Constraint: No technical knowledge about advanced artificial intelligence, machine
learning, databases, or SQL is required. A clear, logic-based, in-memory approach is expected
and fully sufficient to complete this challenge.

• Assignments must respect eligibility, seniority restrictions, and maximum representative
capacities. Critical leads must never be assigned to Junior representatives.
• Teams must be able to explain and defend their scoring choices, fallback behavior, allocation
strategy, and dashboard design.
Optional or bonus objectives
• Incorporate machine learning or other advanced methods to improve the system, provided the
required rules and safeguards remain clear and functional.
• Add useful dashboard controls, analytics, or performance optimizations beyond the core
requirements.

Recommended starting points
• Review the sample datasets and Data Dictionary to identify field formats, aliases, and edge
cases before implementing routing logic.
• Use the Scoring Logic Cheat Sheet to establish the initial priority model, then test VIP
overrides, seniority rules, capacity limits, and overflow cases.
• Build and test the scoring and routing pipeline before connecting it to the dashboard. Consider
how to explain the data flow in a simple system architecture diagram.
• Test the full pipeline on the scale datasets and measure how quickly it processes a large
upload.

Deliverables
Teams should submit:
   1. Closing Presentation (main deliverable): A presentation explaining your team's
      approach and trade-offs, including a short live demonstration of the working software
      within the presentation time. Include the assumptions, data-cleaning and scoring logic,
      routing decisions, fallback strategies, dashboard design, performance results, and
      rationale. The presentation must also contain a System Blueprint, with one or two slides
      showing a visual map of how the different parts of the software connect and move data.
   2. Working Software: The final code, including a brief guide (README.md) on how to set
      it up, install dependencies, and run it locally. Teams may also submit their work by
      pushing the final codebase to a Git repository (e.g., GitHub, GitLab) for easy access and
      review.
   3. Quality Checks: Automated tests (e.g., using pytest) to prove the system works
      correctly. Focus on testing edge cases (missing data, ties in rep capacity, invalid inputs).

A separate written report is not required. Technical explanations and design rationale should be
included in the final presentation.
Presentation format and deadline
Each team will have 10 minutes to present, including any live demonstration, followed by 5
minutes of Q&A. All required deliverables are due Saturday, November 21, 2026, at 10:00 PM
CST.

Evaluation priorities
• Core logic and routing accuracy
• Scale and performance
• Edge case handling and system resilience
• Dashboard usability and functionality
• Presentation, system architecture, and live demonstration

File Glossary
The following supporting files will be provided separately. Teams should use them when
building and evaluating their solutions.
• problem_statement.pdf
     ○ Purpose: The primary document outlining the challenge background, core logic
     requirements, UI constraints, and expected deliverables.
• scoring_logic_cheat_sheet.pdf
     ○ Purpose: The business rules used to calculate priority scores, handle VIP overrides, and
     route leads based on representative seniority.
• data_dictionary.pdf
     ○ Purpose: The schema guide detailing column names, data types, normalization
     requirements (handling aliases and mixed casing), and expected values for both the leads
     and representatives datasets.
• assessment_rubric.pdf
     ○ Purpose: The judging criteria used to evaluate core logic, system scale and performance,
     edge case handling, UI execution, and the final presentation.
• leads_sample.csv
     ○ Purpose: A small dataset of prospective customers for initial development, testing, and
     debugging of the routing logic.
• reps_sample.csv
     ○ Purpose: A small dataset of sales representatives to pair with leads_sample.csv for initial
     testing and validation.
• leads_scale.csv
     ○ Purpose: A large dataset of 50,000 leads used for the Scale Challenge to test high-
     volume processing without freezing.
• reps_scale.csv
     ○ Purpose: A larger directory of 1,600 representatives used with leads_scale.csv to test
     capacity, load balancing, and round-robin distribution.
